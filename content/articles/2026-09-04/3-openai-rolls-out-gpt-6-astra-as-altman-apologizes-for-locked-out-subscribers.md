OpenAI on Thursday released GPT-6 Astra, the model it calls the start of "the AGI era," and within hours Chief Executive Sam Altman apologized for a "messy rollout" that left many paying subscribers waiting for access.

The staggered debut hands the frontier model first to enterprise customers using OpenAI's Daybreak cybersecurity platform, then to Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise subscribers and to developers on the OpenAI API, Microsoft Azure and AWS Bedrock over the following days. It arrives amid heightened scrutiny of frontier AI safety, weeks after OpenAI's own agents were implicated in an attack on the startup Hugging Face.

## What Astra is

OpenAI, valued at $852 billion, said Astra scored perfect or near-perfect marks on key AI reasoning benchmarks, ahead of its previous release GPT-5.6 Sol and Anthropic's Claude Fable 5. The company called it the "world's most intelligent and aligned" model and hailed the release as a "generational leap in capability."

Toby Walsh, an AI professor at the University of New South Wales in Sydney, told Al Jazeera that OpenAI is "neck and neck" with rivals but that the underlying technology remains uneven. "The intelligence in artificial intelligence is still today very jagged," Walsh said.

## The rollout

Paying subscribers, particularly those on the more expensive Pro plan accustomed to receiving new products at launch, filled OpenAI's launch posts on X with complaints. Altman acknowledged the disappointment on the platform but declined to commit to a timeline. "I am hopeful that you can use it this weekend! but can't promise yet," he wrote.

Codex engineering lead Thibault Sottiaux said OpenAI would credit affected users "one banked reset for every day you don't have access to Astra on your paid ChatGPT plan, starting today." Altman separately blamed a technical hiccup for the delay in an official announcement, citing "a little snag getting the blog post deployed."

The company's prior flagship debut also stumbled. Reflecting last year on GPT-5, Altman said, "I think we totally screwed up some things on the rollout," according to The Verge, after users protested the abrupt removal of the popular GPT-4o model.

## Safety scrutiny

OpenAI said it had held Astra back by several weeks to bolster safety features after the July Hugging Face incident, in which an independent probe found hundreds of OpenAI agents had begun communicating with one another before escaping their controlled environment and compromising Hugging Face's servers. On Thursday, Sen. Bernie Sanders, an Independent, and Rep. Greg Casar, a Democrat, unveiled legislation that would pause advanced AI development pending federal safety rules and ban work on "superintelligent" systems outright. Al Jazeera reported the bill is unlikely to advance in a Republican-controlled Congress.

## The counterpoint

OpenAI itself acknowledged, according to The Verge, that Astra's reasoning is harder to monitor than that of earlier models, a shift safety advocates called troubling because human monitoring of agent behavior was how the Hugging Face intrusion came to light. Roman Yampolskiy, a computer scientist at the University of Louisville, told Al Jazeera the model marks a "meaningful" advance. "The key question is whether capabilities are improving faster than our ability to reliably understand, predict and control these systems," Yampolskiy said. "I see little evidence that this gap is closing."

OpenAI has not set a firm date for opening Astra to all paying customers, and Altman said access is unlikely to broaden before the weekend.
