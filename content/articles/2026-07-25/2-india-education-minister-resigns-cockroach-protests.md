NEW DELHI — India's education minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned Saturday after weeks of student-led "Cockroach" protests over exam-paper leaks, handing Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government its first major concession to sustained public dissent since the 2021 reversal of the farm laws.

Pradhan's exit answered the central demand of the Cockroach Janta Party, a satirical youth movement that has built a nationwide sit-in at New Delhi's Jantar Mantar protest site, spread demonstrations to Kolkata, Mumbai and Pune, and turned pop-culture memes into a rolling campaign against a prime minister whose political brand was built on control of the online narrative. Modi has bowed to street pressure only once in more than 12 years in office, when his government withdrew controversial farm laws after a yearlong farmers' protest in 2021.

## The announcement

Pradhan disclosed the decision in a post on X on Saturday, hours before Cockroach leaders were due to meet federal ministers for a second round of talks and as police fired tear gas at protesters near the sit-in, according to Al Jazeera. Authorities cut internet access around the site and closed 18 nearby metro stations.

Pradhan wrote that he was submitting his resignation to Modi so that "antinational forces do not take advantage of this situation" and said he respected the aspirations and legitimate expectations of India's youth.

Prachi Kumar, a corporate employee at the New Delhi protest site, told the Associated Press that Pradhan's departure was overdue. "This had to be done. Someone must be held accountable," she said. The Cockroach Janta Party posted on X: "DEMOCRACY WINS!"

## The movement

The Cockroach Janta Party formed two months ago as a satire group and organized around a series of leaks in national entrance exams, including tests for the country's medical-college admissions program. Modi's government had earlier tried to defuse the anger by announcing fast-track courts for leak cases and drafting legislation to strengthen anti-cheating measures, but protesters rejected the steps as failing to address ministerial accountability.

Tensions escalated Monday, when Delhi police used baton charges, tear gas and, according to a BBC Verify analysis of protest footage, so-called pellet guns to break up a march on parliament. The Central Reserve Police Force told Indian media it was "premature" to comment on the pellet-gun videos and said it was reviewing the events. Nearly half of India's population is under 25, according to the AP, and the Monday crackdown drew larger crowds to Delhi, Kolkata and Mumbai later in the week.

## What's next

Abhijeet Dipke, the Cockroach Janta Party founder, told supporters at Jantar Mantar that the campaign would continue. The group said three demands remain outstanding: one crore rupees, about $103,554, in compensation for each family of a student who died by suicide after alleged exam leaks; no punitive action against demonstrators; and a public apology from Delhi Police and the Rapid Action Force paramilitary over Monday's injuries.

"We won’t go like this," Dipke said, according to Al Jazeera. "Remember, do not mess with cockroach."

## The absent side

Modi and senior Bharatiya Janata Party figures had not publicly addressed the resignation by Saturday afternoon in the reporting available. Neither the prime minister's office nor the Central Reserve Police Force offered a defense of the Monday police action or an explanation for Pradhan's departure. Today's account draws on AP, Al Jazeera, the BBC and the Guardian; pro-government Indian outlets were not represented in the material used for this article, and the government's own framing of the concession is not yet on the record.

The second round of Cockroach-government talks had been scheduled for Saturday. Al Jazeera reported it was unclear whether that meeting would still take place after Pradhan's announcement.
