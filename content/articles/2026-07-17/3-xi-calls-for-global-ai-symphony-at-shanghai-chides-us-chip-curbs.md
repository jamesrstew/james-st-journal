Chinese President Xi Jinping opened the World Artificial Intelligence Conference in Shanghai on Friday with a keynote urging developing nations to help build a China-led alternative to U.S.-dominated AI, telling delegates the technology "should not be a solo performance by a single country, but a symphony of international cooperation."

The address, Xi's first in-person appearance at the four-day annual summit, doubled as his sharpest public rebuke of American export controls that have blocked Chinese firms from the most advanced U.S. AI chips. Xi announced Chinese partnerships with Asian, African, Latin American and BRICS nations to broaden developing-country access to AI, framing the effort as necessary to prevent "new historical injustices."

## A new bloc in Shanghai

On Thursday, representatives from 29 nations signed an agreement to establish the World AI Cooperation Organization, headquartered in Shanghai, China's Xinhua news agency reported. Signatories included Russia, Belarus, Serbia, Cuba, Brazil and Venezuela, along with 12 Asian and 10 African countries. Kazakhstan's leader, Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet and Thailand's Anutin Charnvirakul joined U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres in the Shanghai hall Friday.

Xi's most pointed line targeted Washington's chip regime without naming it. "We should jointly oppose overstretching the national security concept in the field of AI or placing one country's security over that of others," he said. In May, the U.S. Commerce Department reaffirmed that its licensing requirements for advanced AI chip exports apply to every business with a Chinese parent, closing loopholes American officials said had allowed diversion through overseas subsidiaries.

## Tokens, chips and power

Chinese AI usage is growing on a scale that lends weight to Beijing's pitch. Daily consumption of "tokens" — the industry unit of AI usage — has increased a thousandfold over the past two years, according to Chinese state media citing officials. Chinese AI models are drawing global users with lower costs than U.S. offerings even as Beijing pours state money into a domestic ecosystem stretching from chip production to consumer apps.

China lags the United States in access to the most advanced semiconductors but leads in the electricity that AI data centers demand. A typical data center consumes as much power as 100,000 households, while next-generation "hyperscale" facilities can draw as much as 2 million homes, according to the International Energy Agency. China already generates more than twice as much electricity as the United States, a lead the IEA expects to widen amid state-led grid investment.

Xi also stressed a "people-centered" approach, pledging that Beijing would put in place laws, regulations and emergency response systems to "ensure AI is always under human control."

## The counterpoint

The White House and Commerce Department had not publicly responded to Xi's implicit criticism of U.S. chip curbs by the close of Friday's on-the-record reporting. Washington and Brussels have justified their advanced-chip export restrictions on national security grounds, citing concerns about AI's military applications and misuse by hackers and criminals.

The four-day conference runs through Monday and features more than 1,000 Chinese exhibitors displaying 3,000 products, including a smartphone that can operate apps autonomously. The gathering precedes the first government-level AI talks between Beijing and Washington under the Trump administration; no date has been announced.
