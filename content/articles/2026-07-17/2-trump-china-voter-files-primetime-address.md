President Trump used a primetime address from the White House East Room on Thursday to accuse China of orchestrating "the largest compromise of election data in history" during the 2020 vote, alleging Beijing acquired 220 million U.S. voter files and calling on Congress to pass the SAVE America Act, which would ban most mail-in voting.

The 25-minute speech, delivered three months before November's midterm elections, marked Trump's most direct effort to relitigate his 2020 loss from the White House podium since returning to power, and extended a campaign to push the SAVE Act through a Senate that has stalled it for months. Trump withheld his signature from the bipartisan housing bill this month to pressure lawmakers, and fired the three remaining commissioners of the U.S. Election Assistance Commission the week before.

Trump said the White House had declassified hundreds of intelligence documents purporting to show that Chinese operatives had "bought, stolen or hacked" voter data in 18 states starting in 2018, and that officials at the FBI, CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence had suppressed the findings. He directed those agencies to investigate the alleged cover-up "and to file criminal charges, if appropriate."

## What Trump alleged

Trump cited a CIA report he said described a Chinese strategy to "leverage all domestic and foreign elements that were opposed to the U.S. president" during his first term, and alleged that raw FBI intelligence from 2020 pointed to a Chinese attempt to "manufacture illegal ballots for Joe Biden." He said the author of the CIA report is now "under review." John Solomon, a White House special adviser who helped compile the documents, told reporters outside the White House that the released material contained no evidence that foreign actors had flipped a single 2020 vote.

Trump also said the Department of Homeland Security had identified 278,000 non-citizens on state voter rolls, without indicating how many, if any, had voted. He said the Homeland Security secretary would hold a briefing Friday to outline confirmed cyber vulnerabilities in electronic voting systems.

## What the record shows

A 2021 assessment by the National Intelligence Council concluded with "high confidence" that China did not deploy interference efforts to change the 2020 outcome, judging that Beijing viewed neither a Trump nor a Biden victory as advantageous enough to risk exposure. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency at the time called the vote "the most secure in American history." A 2022 CISA review of Dominion Voting Systems machines identified vulnerabilities the agency said could be exploited only "under very specific conditions." CBS News fact-checkers noted that U.S. voting machines are not connected to the internet and are backed by paper ballots that permit hand audits.

More than 60 lawsuits filed by Trump and his allies contesting the 2020 result were dismissed by state and federal judges, including several appointed by Republican presidents.

## The counterpoint

China's foreign ministry called the allegations "entirely fabricated" and "malicious smears," spokesman Lin Jian said. The Chinese embassy in Washington told CNN that Beijing "has all along adhered to the principle of non-interference in others' internal affairs." Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said, "Let's be clear - in America, voters choose their leaders, not the other way around." Sen. Mark Warner of Virginia, the vice chairman of the Senate Intelligence Committee, said the intelligence community, FBI, Homeland Security, Justice Department, courts and bipartisan state officials had all rejected the president's claims over years of review. "The facts have not changed," Warner said.

ABC, NBC and CNN declined to air the speech on their primary broadcast channels, streaming it on their websites and secondary services instead. Trump called for the networks' broadcast licenses to be revoked. His longtime teleprompter operator was placed on administrative leave hours before airtime after the White House said he had wagered nearly $100,000 on a prediction market over what the president would say, press secretary Karoline Leavitt said.

Chinese President Xi Jinping has been invited to Washington in September, months after Trump traveled to China in May to meet him.
