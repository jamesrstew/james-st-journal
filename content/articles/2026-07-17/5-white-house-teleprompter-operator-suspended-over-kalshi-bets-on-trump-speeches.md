The White House placed teleprompter operator Gabriel Perez on unpaid administrative leave Thursday after Kalshi's compliance team flagged approximately $100,000 in prediction-market bets tied to what President Trump would say during his speeches.

The suspension of Perez, on Trump's staff since 2016, delivers the largest insider-trading test case to date for the fast-growing prediction-market industry and its federal overseer. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has begun settlement discussions with Perez, ABC News reported; federal prosecutors in Manhattan declined to open a criminal case. Similar matters over the past year have snared a Google employee, a special forces soldier accused of betting on the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro and former Rep. George Santos, accused last month of wagering on his own State of the Union attendance.

## The mentions market

Perez wagered on Kalshi 'mentions' contracts — bets on whether a speaker will use specified words or phrases during a public event — across more than a dozen Trump addresses this year, ABC News reported. The events included the February State of the Union, a Medal of Honor ceremony and Trump's remarks at the World Economic Forum in Davos. Kalshi froze more than $90,000 in unwithdrawn profits, the platform told the BBC.

Robert DeNault, Kalshi's head of enforcement, said the exchange's "surveillance team promptly flagged and referred these trades" to the CFTC after an internal review. Kalshi said its analysts first noticed unusual activity in March and traced the account, through employment data collected under a disclosure rule adopted last month, to a federal employee operating White House teleprompters.

## White House response

Press secretary Karoline Leavitt, in her first briefing since returning from maternity leave, said Trump considered the conduct "deeply unfortunate and frankly a disgrace." She initially described the leave as paid before correcting herself and adding that Perez "will no longer be working at the White House." Government records show Perez earned $175,000 a year as a deputy assistant to the president and technical adviser. CFTC spokesperson Zach Fulton said the agency could not confirm or deny an investigation. Sources told the BBC Perez has been "fully cooperative" with regulators.

## The regulator question

Leavitt told reporters she thought the episode "played out exactly how it should," citing Kalshi's self-referral as evidence that industry safeguards work. That reading is not universal. Two senators introduced legislation this year that would narrow the prediction-market industry, prompting Kalshi and Polymarket to add surveillance tools and user-disclosure rules in March. CFTC Chairman Michael Selig has publicly sided with Kalshi in its state-court gambling fights, arguing federal law pre-empts state statutes on prediction markets.

Trump delivered a Thursday-night primetime address on election integrity with a substitute at the teleprompter. Kalshi had multiple markets open on what he would say and how long he would speak.
