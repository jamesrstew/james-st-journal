U.S. warplanes struck six bridges in Iran's southern Hormozgan province, Iranshahr Airport in the country's southeast and a railway station Thursday night, extending the American air campaign into a sixth consecutive night and widening the target list beyond the Bandar Abbas port hit in Wednesday's overnight wave. Provincial authorities said seven people were killed.

The raids carry through President Trump's April threat to bomb Iranian bridges and power plants if Tehran did not return to nuclear talks. They deepen a war both governments said they had halted in June, keep the Strait of Hormuz shut to global shipping and make U.S. bombing of Iranian civilian infrastructure — which the U.N. human rights office has said constitutes a war crime — sustained American policy.

## What was hit

BBC Verify confirmed damage to the Gariveh Bridge in Hormozgan, with night footage showing flames on the span and daylight images showing rubble around a broken road, the BBC reported. Iranian state media and local officials said the raid also struck five other bridges, Iranshahr Airport and a rail station. U.S. Central Command said the attacks were intended to "further degrade Iranian military capabilities" and declined to publish a target list. Iranian officials say the six-day campaign has killed more than 35 people and wounded 300, the Associated Press reported.

## The counterstrike

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it retaliated by hitting U.S. maritime surveillance radar in Oman and targets in Kuwait and Bahrain, and by attacking a U.S. special-operations base at al-Tanf in eastern Syria. Neither Washington nor Damascus confirmed the al-Tanf claim. Kuwaiti authorities said Iranian missiles hit a power plant and a water desalination station. The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations agency said an unidentified projectile struck a tanker sailing near Khasab, Oman, on Thursday; the crew was reported safe. Iran also launched new missiles at Qatar, a mediator in the war, the AP reported.

## Trump's line

In a primetime address, Trump told Americans, "We are likewise winning big in Iran, and you will see the fruits of that labor very, very shortly," the AP reported. In a Fox Business interview this week, the president left open a ground escalation, saying he would "see what's happening" when asked whether he might "wipe out" the Revolutionary Guard "like you did ISIS," according to the Washington Examiner. Trump added that Tehran had asked to meet: "They always want to meet."

Al Jazeera characterized the U.S. raids as fulfilling threats "to bomb civilian infrastructure," a framing Iranian state media and U.N. human rights chief Volker Türk have echoed. Türk has said "deliberately attacking civilians and civilian infrastructure is a war crime." Central Command has offered no rebuttal beyond its degrade-capabilities statement.

The Strait of Hormuz — which handled about a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas before Tehran shut it in February — remained closed Friday. "We should be worried, and I am worried, if the situation does not improve in the next few weeks," International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol said Thursday.
