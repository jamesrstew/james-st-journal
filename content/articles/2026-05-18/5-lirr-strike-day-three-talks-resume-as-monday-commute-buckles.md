A late-night meeting between the Metropolitan Transportation Authority and the five unions striking the Long Island Rail Road broke up around 1:30 a.m. Monday without a deal, the National Mediation Board said, leaving the busiest commuter railroad in North America shut down for a third day and 250,000 weekday riders to scramble for a way into New York City.

The mediation board, which had summoned both sides Sunday evening, said the parties would return to the table at 7:30 a.m. Monday. Shuttle buses from six stations in Nassau and Suffolk counties to subway connections in Queens began rolling at 4 a.m., the MTA said, with capacity for about 13,000 daily riders against the system's normal 250,000.

## What is new

Sunday's session was the first formal bargaining since talks collapsed Friday night, hours before the walkout began at 12:01 a.m. Saturday. Gov. Kathy Hochul, a Democrat, used a Sunday news conference to summon the unions back.

"This is my official invitation. We didn't want you to leave. You left. You're welcome to come back. I'll provide refreshments, whatever you like. Just c'mon back," Hochul said, according to the Associated Press.

Hochul put the cost of the shutdown at $61 million per day. MTA Chairman and Chief Executive Janno Lieber said the unions walked out after the agency moved on wages.

"We are more than willing to meet them halfway in wages. In fact, more than halfway, which is why we put new ideas on the table again and again," Lieber told reporters.

## The gap

Union officials said the MTA had agreed to 9.5 percent wage increases over the first three years of a four-year contract and that the two sides were within roughly one percentage point on the fourth year when the agency introduced a demand to lift healthcare contributions for new hires from 2 percent to 10 percent.

"We're looking for a modest raise in our wages," said Raymond Delio, secretary and treasurer for the Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers and Trainmen. "Health insurance should never be on the table. We work hard enough."

The coalition represents about 3,500 LIRR workers in the railroad's first walkout since a two-day strike in 1994.

## The commute

Even a Monday deal would not restore service immediately. CBS News reported the railroad needs at least a day to inspect tracks, signals and equipment and to position crews before full operations resume.

Former Transportation Commissioner Sam Schwartz told CBS the disruption would reach far beyond the rail platform. "Those from the South Shore are going to see the Belt Parkway backed up like they haven't seen in a long time. The Gowanus will be impacted. Buses will be impacted," he said. State and city officials repeatedly urged commuters who can work from home to do so.

## The counterpoint

Lieber said the LIRR unions are the highest-paid railway workers in the country, with many members earning well into six figures, and called their demands an attempt to force a bad settlement. Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman, a Republican challenging Hochul this year, called on the governor to suspend Manhattan congestion pricing tolls for the duration of the strike.

The board session resumes at 7:30 a.m. Monday. Picket lines were back up at LIRR stations and outside Penn Station.
