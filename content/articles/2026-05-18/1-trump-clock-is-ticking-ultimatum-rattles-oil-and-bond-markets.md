President Trump told Iran in a Truth Social post Sunday that "For Iran, the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them," and Fox News reported Monday that he would convene a top-level Situation Room meeting on Iran with no nuclear deal in sight, sending Brent crude back toward $110 a barrel and pushing long-dated Treasury yields sharply higher at the New York open.

The weekend ultimatum, paired with a White House agenda that no longer includes a near-term nuclear deal and with renewed Iranian airstrikes on Gulf partners that drew European condemnation, has yanked the Hormuz war back to the front of the trading book a week after the president left Beijing without a resolution. Energy, rates and equity futures are now pricing the same risk: that the fragile April ceasefire gives way to a fourth month of fighting and a tighter physical oil market.

## What moved

International benchmark Brent crude futures for July rose 0.64 percent to $109.96 a barrel in early Monday trade, CNBC reported, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for June advanced 0.55 percent to $106. The move extended a run that has kept crude above $100 a barrel through the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which, before the war, carried nearly a fifth of the world's oil and gas supply. Long-dated U.S. Treasury yields, which had broken to multiyear highs late last week amid mounting inflation concern, opened higher again Monday and dragged S&P 500 futures lower with them.

Trump's post added "TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!" and was followed within hours by the disclosure of the Situation Room session, which Fox News listed Monday as the lead item in its morning headlines roundup under the line "Trump to convene top-level Situation Room meeting on Iran with no nuclear deal in sight." Fox separately reported that the president and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had spoken "amid reports of potential revival of military action on Iran."

## On the supply side

The International Energy Agency, in its latest monthly update, cautioned that oil inventories globally are depleting at a record pace as the Strait of Hormuz remains closed. "Rapidly shrinking buffers amid continued disruptions, may herald future price spikes ahead," the agency said. Swiss bank UBS estimated last week that inventories will near all-time lows of 7.6 billion barrels by the end of May if demand holds flat.

Jeff Currie, executive co-chairman at Abaxx Commodity Exchange, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Monday that the picture in the physical market is worse than the screen prices suggest. "Anybody who gets their hands dirty in this business is telling you this is bad," Currie said. "The Iranians want to inflict pain. It's not the price of oil that matters here — it's the availability of oil." Currie said there was "no physical shortage of oil yet" but that Europe could hit one by the end of the month and that the U.S. would feel it by the start of the summer driving season.

## The Gulf flashpoint

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Monday issued a short statement condemning "renewed Iranian airstrikes against the United Arab Emirates and other partners," CBS News reported, calling on Tehran to "enter into serious negotiations with the USA, stop threatening its neighbors."

Iran's Foreign Ministry pushed back on the shipping picture. Spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei told reporters in Tehran that Iran was "committed to ensuring that transit through the Strait of Hormuz is conducted in the safest possible manner" and was "in continuous contact with Oman and other relevant parties to develop a mechanism" to do so. The head of the Iranian parliament's national security commission said over the weekend that Tehran had prepared a plan to "manage traffic" in the strait under which cooperating commercial vessels would pay "necessary fees." CBS, citing a Bloomberg report, said almost two dozen tankers were sitting in and around Iran's main oil export hub of Kharg Island on Monday, which analysts said the regime had begun using as a "toll booth" weeks ago.

The Israel Defense Forces, separately, issued another warning Monday to civilians in three southern Lebanon villages to evacuate, accusing Hezbollah of violating the U.S.-brokered ceasefire and warning of imminent strikes, CBS reported. Israel had agreed only this weekend to extend that ceasefire by 45 days.

## The counterpoint

Not every account framed the morning as a price story. CBS News led its Monday coverage with the renewed Israeli evacuation orders in southern Lebanon and questioned whether the U.S., as Trump claimed, is actually in control of the strait, noting traffic remained "virtually gridlocked" there. The implication, contested by the administration, is that Sunday's post was rhetoric grafted onto a status quo the White House does not in fact dictate, and that traders who took the ultimatum at face value Monday are paying for a policy shift that has not yet happened. Fox News, for its part, foregrounded that no nuclear deal is in sight — a framing that cuts the other way, suggesting the diplomatic track itself, not just the price tape, has run out of road.

The next test arrives Monday afternoon, when the Situation Room meeting breaks and the White House signals whether the ultimatum is a prelude to action or another turn of the screw. The June WTI contract settles at 2:30 p.m. Eastern.
