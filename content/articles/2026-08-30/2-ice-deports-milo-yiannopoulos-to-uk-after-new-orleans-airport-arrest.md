NEW ORLEANS — Immigration and Customs Enforcement deported Milo Yiannopoulos, the British right-wing commentator and longtime advocate of mass deportations, to the United Kingdom on Friday, a day after arresting him at Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport for overstaying his visa, the Department of Homeland Security said Saturday.

The removal of a former Breitbart editor and early Trump supporter turned into a set piece for the president's second-term immigration campaign, as DHS paired Yiannopoulos's mug shot with a pitch for its voluntary self-deportation program. That gave the agency both a recognizable face for its enforcement push and a target whose own record of calling for on-the-spot removals had put him squarely on the side of the policy now used to expel him.

## The arrest

Yiannopoulos, 41, entered the U.S. legally in May 2019 and stayed after his visa lapsed, DHS said. An immigration judge issued a final order of removal on July 22 after he failed to appear at a hearing. ICE agents took him into custody Thursday at the New Orleans airport. NBC News reported that Ye, the rapper formerly known as Kanye West, performed at the Caesars Superdome on Friday evening, and said it is not clear whether Yiannopoulos was in the city for the concert. Yiannopoulos had worked on the rapper's unsuccessful 2020 presidential bid.

A DHS spokesperson told the BBC that Yiannopoulos, "an illegal alien from the United Kingdom," had been sent "back to his home country" and had "chose to overstay his welcome in violation of our nation's laws."

## The messaging

The department made the arrest a marketing moment. "DHS reversed the horrendous Biden-era policy that allowed aliens in our country illegally to jet around our country. Under President Trump, DHS will no longer tolerate this," a DHS spokesperson said in a statement reported by CBS News, adding that the administration will ensure people in the country illegally "can no longer fly unless it is out of our country to self-deport." On X, the department posted the mug shot above the line "Being in detention is a choice" and pointed viewers to the CBP Home App, which NBC News said offers undocumented immigrants $3,000 and a free flight home to leave voluntarily.

Laura Loomer, a Trump ally who has feuded with Yiannopoulos, claimed on X Friday night that she had reported him to ICE. "This subversive foreign agent is already out of the United States and DHS tells me he's NEVER allowed to come back to the United States ever again," Loomer wrote, the BBC reported.

## His own words

Yiannopoulos built much of his post-Breitbart brand around hard-line immigration positions. The BBC reported that he wrote on social media in 2025: "We have got to deport millions and millions of people. Without that, nothing else matters." Last year he called for "total legal immunity for ICE agents when they're on duty."

"California needs ICE checkpoints at supermarket entrances, gas stations, strip malls, intersections and government buildings, with on the spot deportation for anyone who can't prove they are in the US legally," he wrote in a June 2025 post cited by CBS News.

## The counterpoint

Yiannopoulos and his representatives had not publicly responded to the removal by press time, and no due-process objection to the July 22 in-absentia order has surfaced in the reporting. The four outlets that carried the deportation — the BBC, CBS News, NBC News and The Verge — are all center or lean-left; right-of-center voices sympathetic to Yiannopoulos, few after his split with much of the movement, have not weighed in on the record.

Loomer said DHS told her Yiannopoulos would "NEVER" be permitted to re-enter the United States. On Friday, he was on a plane home.
