Firefighters working an overnight operation inside the GKN Aerospace plant in Garden Grove, Calif., may have found a crack in the leaking chemical tank that has held Orange County in evacuation since Thursday, Orange County Fire Authority interim chief T.J. McGovern said Sunday, a finding he said "could potentially be relieving some of the pressure in there." McGovern called it "a step in the right direction."

The potential crack, if confirmed, would relieve the pressure build inside a tank holding roughly 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate and ease the scenario fire officials have warned about all weekend: a Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion, or BLEVE, that could send the highly flammable chemical airborne over a populated stretch of Orange County. Saturday's update from Gov. Gavin Newsom, who declared a state of emergency for the county, had assumed the tank would fail. Roughly 50,000 residents across Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster remain under mandatory evacuation orders, according to the Federal Emergency Management Agency.

## What changed overnight

Crews entered the danger zone late Saturday and worked through the night. McGovern said Sunday that a second mission would begin Sunday night and run until morning. "It'll be an all-night mission in order to confirm that the pressure in the tank has been released and that the BLEVE [Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion] threat is eliminated," he said. He added: "The BLEVE threat is the worst-case catastrophic event that we've been talking about. We are not there yet."

The pressure inside the tank had been the central problem. Orange County Fire Authority incident commander Craig Covey said the internal temperature climbed about 1 degree per hour through Friday, reaching 90 degrees Friday night after starting the day at 77 and topping 100 degrees by Saturday night, more than double what Covey has called the chemical's "happy place." A passive crack venting vapor would short-circuit that climb.

## The unresolved valve

FEMA struck a more cautious note earlier Sunday. In a morning update, the agency said the malfunctioning valve at the heart of the leak "remains unmitigated as the chemical reaction continues without a way to neutralize it." Two adjacent tanks at the facility were affected but were deemed structurally sound as of that update.

Methyl methacrylate, used to make aerospace-grade plastics, generates heat on its own and ignites readily. Elias Picazo, an associate professor of chemistry at the University of Southern California, told CBS News that any spark or flash near vented vapor could touch off an uncontrolled fire or explosion. Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the Orange County health officer, has warned that exposure can cause respiratory irritation, eye and skin burning, nausea and headaches. No injuries or deaths have been reported.

## On the ground

Garden Grove sits about 38 miles south of downtown Los Angeles and abuts Anaheim, home to the Disneyland parks, which remain outside the evacuation zone. McGovern on Sunday night asked residents to keep clear of the cordon: "Please help us. Stay out of the evacuation zone. Let us run our operation."

Danny Pham, a restaurant worker who lives two blocks from the plant, told the Associated Press his roommate banged on his door at 7 a.m. Friday. He grabbed his wallet and passport and drove to a friend's restaurant in a neighboring city. Kim Yen, a retiree, said she worried that Vietnamese-speaking neighbors might miss the English-language alerts.

## The risk that remains

A confirmed crack is not a fix. Covey said Friday the tank could still fail in either of two directions: a ground spill of thousands of gallons of methyl methacrylate, which is heavier than air and would pool and sink, or an explosion that would also affect surrounding tanks holding chemicals and fuel. Crews have laid sandbag containment barriers to keep any spill out of storm drains and waterways. "Letting this thing just fail and blow up is unacceptable to us," Covey said earlier in the week.

Fire officials said they expect to know by Monday morning whether Sunday night's mission confirms the pressure has been released. The cause of the original leak remains under investigation.
