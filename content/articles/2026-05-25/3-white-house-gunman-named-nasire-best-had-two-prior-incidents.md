Law enforcement sources on Sunday identified the man killed Saturday evening outside the White House as Nasire Best, 21, of Dundalk, Maryland, and disclosed that Secret Service agents had detained him twice in 2025 after he tried to breach the complex and told officers he was Jesus Christ. A D.C. bench warrant for failing to appear in court was active when Best drew a revolver at the 17th Street checkpoint and opened fire.

The disclosures, drawn from D.C. Superior Court records and officials cited by CBS News and the Associated Press, recast Saturday's shooting as the failed end of a case the court system had tracked for nearly a year. The gunfire was also the third shooting incident near President Trump in roughly a month, according to PBS NewsHour, raising questions about how a man under a stay-away order and an open warrant reached the same checkpoint a third time.

## The court file

Court documents obtained by CBS News show Best blocked a White House entry lane in June 2025, told agents he was Jesus Christ and said he wanted to be arrested. He was sent for a mental evaluation. In July 2025 he returned and again attempted to enter the area around the White House. Secret Service agents arrested him on a charge of unlawfully entering a federally controlled property in Washington, and a judge ordered him to stay away.

Best was released after his arraignment but failed to appear for an Aug. 7, 2025, status hearing, CBS News reported, citing D.C. Superior Court records. That triggered a no-bond, D.C.-only bench warrant authorizing any officer in the District to arrest him on sight. PBS NewsHour, citing the AP, reported Best did appear at a later hearing, but the August bench warrant remained the operative record.

## Saturday's sequence

Best reappeared at 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW around 6 p.m. Saturday, pulled a revolver from a messenger bag and opened fire at the Secret Service checkpoint, law enforcement sources told CBS News. Multiple uniformed division officers, including at least one in a cruiser outside the checkpoint, returned fire and killed him. A bystander was wounded; investigators have not determined whether the round came from Best or from responding officers. The bystander underwent successful surgery and remained in serious but stable condition Sunday.

No Secret Service officers were injured. Trump was not impacted, the agency said, and had stayed at the White House after canceling a Friday plan to travel to his New Jersey golf club.

## Third incident in a month

Saturday's shooting was the third near Trump or his protective detail in about a month, PBS NewsHour reported. On April 25, Cole Tomas Allen of Torrance, California, allegedly ran through a checkpoint at the White House Correspondents' Association Dinner and fired a shotgun at a Secret Service officer; he has pleaded not guilty to charges of attempting to kill the president. On May 4, Secret Service officers shot Michael Marx, 45, of Midland, Texas, near the Washington Monument, wounding a teenage bystander. Best's death is the first of the three to end in a suspect fatality.

Secret Service Director Sean M. Curran said 'Our officers continue to operate heroically in a heightened political threat environment.' The Metropolitan Police Department will lead the use-of-force investigation, the standard process when Secret Service personnel open fire in the District. FBI Director Kash Patel said on social media that bureau personnel were on scene and 'we will update the public as we're able.'

## The view not in the file

No member of Best's family, no defense attorney from his 2025 case and no mental-health advocate had spoken on the record by press time; the available reporting relied on law enforcement sources and court filings, leaving the suspect's side unrepresented. The court record establishes a history of mental illness and a prior judicial order for evaluation, but how Best obtained the revolver, where he had been living since the August warrant and whether he was receiving treatment remain unanswered.

The Metropolitan Police Department use-of-force review is expected to take several weeks. Best's underlying 2025 unlawful-entry case will now be closed administratively.
