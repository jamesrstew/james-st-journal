Russia fired an Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile at Ukraine overnight Saturday into Sunday, the third use of the nuclear-capable weapon in the four-year war, as part of a barrage of 600 strike drones and 90 air-, sea- and ground-launched missiles, Ukrainian and Russian officials said.

The scale and the choice of weapon mark one of the heaviest combined attacks since the 2022 invasion and put Kyiv back at the center of a war that had drifted to the eastern front. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said most of the strikes hit the capital and that air defenses failed to intercept every ballistic missile, exposing again a chronic shortage of interceptors capable of downing high-speed warheads.

## What was hit

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said two people were killed in the capital and 81 wounded. The head of the surrounding Kyiv region reported two more dead and nine wounded. Ukraine's emergency service recorded damage at 50 locations across several districts, including residential buildings, shopping centers, schools, a market and police buildings. The cabinet building and the Foreign Ministry took minor damage. Zelenskyy said the Oreshnik itself hit Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region, without specifying the target, and that the Museum of Chernobyl was among the cultural sites struck, with more than 40 percent of its holdings "irretrievably lost."

Ukraine's Air Force said its defenses destroyed or jammed 549 drones and 55 missiles, and that 19 missiles failed to reach their targets.

## Putin's announced retaliation

President Vladimir Putin on Friday ordered the military to draw up retaliation proposals after a drone strike on a college dormitory in Starobilsk, in Russian-occupied Luhansk, that Kremlin-installed authorities said killed 21 people and wounded 42. Putin said no military or law-enforcement facilities sat near the college.

Russia's Defense Ministry on Sunday said it had used Oreshnik, Iskander, Kinzhal and Tsirkon missiles, along with drones, to strike Ukrainian "military command and control facilities," air bases and military industrial enterprises, and cast the attack as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on "civilian facilities on Russian territory." It did not name targets and later said no civilian sites were hit. Ukraine's U.N. ambassador, Andrii Melnyk, rejected war-crimes accusations Moscow has leveled over Starobilsk, calling them a "pure propaganda show."

## A rare weapon

Russia first fired the multiple-warhead Oreshnik at Dnipro in November 2024 and used it again in the Lviv region in January. Putin has said the missile travels at Mach 10 and can destroy bunkers several floors underground. Rollo Collins, an investigator at the Centre for Information Resilience, told Reuters that Sunday's warhead appeared to split into 36 submunitions. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik signaled "a form of escalation and the impasse of Russia's war of aggression."

## The contested frame

Both body sources are lean-left wires drawing on AP, AFP and Reuters. Moscow frames the strike as a proportional answer to Ukrainian targeting of Russian civilians and insists only military sites were hit in Kyiv. The damage logged by Ukrainian authorities — apartment blocks, a market, schools, supermarkets and a metro station foyer — does not match that account, and independent verification of Russian target claims was not available Sunday.

EU foreign ministers are due to meet within days to weigh additional pressure on Moscow, foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said. Ukraine's air-defense shortfall, laid bare again by the Oreshnik, is likely to dominate the agenda.
