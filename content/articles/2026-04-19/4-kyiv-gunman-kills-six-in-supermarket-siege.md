A gunman carrying an automatic weapon killed six people and wounded 14 in central Kyiv on Saturday, shooting four victims on the street before barricading himself with hostages inside a supermarket and being shot dead by police after a 40-minute standoff, Ukrainian officials said.

The attack, in the capital's Holosiivskyi district, was the first mass shooting in Kyiv since Russia's 2022 invasion and forced the city's tactical police, usually trained on drone and missile fallout, into a hostage rescue in the middle of a wartime weekend. Ukraine's security service, the SBU, labeled the killings an act of terrorism.

## What happened

The attacker, whom police did not name, was 58 years old, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in a video address. Zelenskyy said the man was born in Russia, had a prior criminal record and had lived for an extended period in the eastern Donetsk region, parts of which remain occupied by Russian forces.

"It has been established that the attacker set fire to an apartment before taking to the streets with a weapon," Zelenskyy said.

Interior Minister Ihor Klymenko, speaking at the scene in body armor, said the gunman shot four bystanders on the street, then entered a shopping center housing a supermarket and killed a fifth person, a hostage. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said a young woman wounded in the attack died at a hospital, bringing the toll to six.

## The standoff

A female negotiator stationed behind an armored vehicle used a loudspeaker for roughly 40 minutes, urging the gunman to release customers and staff held inside. "The people are not to blame for this. Please let them go, and we will talk with you," she said, according to the Associated Press account carried by NPR.

Klymenko said officers also offered to deliver tourniquets for a person believed wounded inside. "We tried to persuade him, knowing that there was likely a wounded person inside. We even offered to bring in tourniquets to stop the bleeding, but he did not respond," Klymenko said. "Consequently, the order was given to neutralize him." Special tactical units then stormed the store.

## The weapon

Klymenko said the carbine used in the attack was legally registered. The gunman had approached licensing authorities in December 2025 to have the weapon test-fired as his permit was expiring and had submitted a medical certificate with an application to renew. Investigators will now determine which medical institution issued that certificate, Klymenko said.

A neighbor in the attacker's apartment block, 75-year-old Hanna Kulyk, told the Associated Press she recognized him. "I knew him by sight. He seemed like an educated, refined man. You'd never guess he was some kind of criminal," Kulyk said. "He lived alone."

## The caveat

No motive had been made public by Saturday night, and neither wire accounts nor Ukrainian statements tied the shooting to the war with Russia despite the attacker's Russian birth and Donetsk ties. Russian officials had not publicly responded by press time, and the available reporting drew on Ukrainian government sources and Western wires; a Moscow account of the gunman's background is absent from today's record.

Police said the investigation would continue over the weekend, with the SBU's terrorism designation leaving open the possibility of further charges or disclosures about the attacker's network, if any, in the coming days.
