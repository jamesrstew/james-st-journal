North Korea launched multiple short-range ballistic missiles toward the sea on Sunday from its eastern Sinpo area, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, with each weapon flying about 140 kilometers, or 87 miles, before landing in waters off the North's east coast. The launches came days after the United Nations nuclear watchdog warned that Pyongyang was making rapid advances in its weapons program, and hours before South Korean President Lee Jae Myung departed on a state trip to India and Vietnam.

The test is the latest in a steady run of North Korean weapons activity this year and lands at a diplomatically loaded moment. President Donald Trump is scheduled to travel to Beijing in May for a rescheduled summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, a meeting outside observers cited by the Associated Press say Pyongyang may be trying to leverage. Sinpo is also the home of North Korea's main submarine shipyard, and South Korean and Japanese defense officials are still working out whether Sunday's weapons flew from a submarine, a land-based launcher or both.

## What was fired

South Korea's military said the missiles were short-range and flew east, and that Seoul has bolstered its surveillance posture while sharing information with Washington and Tokyo. The South Korean presidential office convened an emergency National Security Council meeting, where senior officials "expressed concerns about North Korea's repeated ballistic missile tests and urged it to stop them immediately," according to the Associated Press account carried by PBS NewsHour.

Japan's Defense Ministry also detected the launches. Tokyo lodged a formal protest with Pyongyang, saying the firings "threaten regional and international peace" and violated U.N. Security Council resolutions that ban any ballistic activity by the North. Deputy Minister of Defense Masahisa Miyazaki told reporters Japan was analyzing launch details in coordination with the United States and South Korea. U.S. Indo-Pacific Command said it remains committed to the defense of the U.S. homeland and its allies in the region.

## The submarine question

If analysts confirm a submarine-launched ballistic missile, it would be North Korea's first such test in four years, the Associated Press reported. Underwater launches are harder for rival militaries to detect in advance. North Korea unveiled a nuclear-powered submarine under construction last year, and the Sinpo yard has long been the site of its sea-based missile program.

Sunday's salvo follows a week in which state media said Kim Jong Un supervised missile tests from a destroyer, and earlier rounds this month involving missiles armed with cluster-bomb warheads. Last month, North Korea said it tested an upgraded solid-fuel engine for missiles capable of reaching the U.S. mainland. International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi said in Seoul on Wednesday his agency has confirmed "a rapid increase" in activities at North Korean nuclear facilities, and warned of "a very serious increase" in weapons production capabilities.

## Diplomatic backdrop

Kim has focused on enlarging his nuclear and missile arsenals since his nuclear diplomacy with Trump collapsed in 2019. Trump has repeatedly expressed his desire to restore those talks, and Kim has recently left open the door to dialogue while demanding Washington drop its call for nuclear disarmament as a precondition. The May summit with Xi could provide what the AP described as a diplomatic opening with Pyongyang.

The White House, the Pentagon and congressional Republicans had not publicly responded to Sunday's launches by press time; the only U.S. comment on the record was the Indo-Pacific Command statement reaffirming treaty commitments.

South Korea's military said it was still assessing the launch platforms late Sunday, with findings expected to be shared with U.S. and Japanese counterparts in the coming days. Trump's Beijing trip in May will be the next major test of whether Pyongyang's escalating weapons calendar pulls it back to the negotiating table, or further from it.
