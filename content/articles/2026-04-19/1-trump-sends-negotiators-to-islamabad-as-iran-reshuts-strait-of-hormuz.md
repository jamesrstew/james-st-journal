President Trump said Sunday that U.S. negotiators will travel to Pakistan on Monday evening for a second round of direct talks with Iran, hours after Tehran reimposed its shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz and Revolutionary Guard gunboats fired on two India-flagged ships trying to cross the waterway. The ceasefire between Washington and Tehran expires Wednesday.

The announcement, made on Truth Social, arrived alongside a threat. If Iran refuses what Trump called "a very fair and reasonable DEAL," the United States, he wrote, "is going to knock out every single Power Plant, and every single Bridge, in Iran." Iran did not immediately confirm the dates, but Pakistani authorities moved overnight to clear central Islamabad, suspending public transport and restricting movement in and out of the Red Zone, the high-security sector that houses most embassies and government buildings.

## What is new

Sunday's moves reversed a brief opening of the strait that Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi had tied on Friday to the 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire. After vessels came under fire in mid-transit on Saturday, ships in the Persian Gulf held their positions. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said control of the strait had "returned to its previous state," blaming what it called U.S. "acts of piracy and maritime theft under the guise of a so-called blockade." The U.S. military said on X that it had forced 23 ships to turn around as part of its cordon of Iranian ports.

That returned the chokepoint, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's seaborne oil and gas, to the pre-ceasefire status quo and reset the clock on the energy shock that drove Brent crude above $100 earlier this month. On Friday, Brent had fallen roughly 10 percent after Araghchi's reopening announcement.

## On the table

Vice President JD Vance led the first round of talks in Islamabad on April 11, a 21-hour session that collapsed without agreement and was followed two days later by the U.S. blockade. Trump has not said publicly which officials he will send this time; the White House and Vance's office did not respond to Associated Press queries on Sunday.

The agenda, by both sides' accounts, has not moved. Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator, Mohammed Bagher Qalibaf, told state television late Saturday: "It is impossible for others to pass through the Strait of Hormuz while we cannot." He said separately there would be "no retreat in the field of diplomacy," while acknowledging the gap between the two sides remained wide. Iran's Supreme National Security Council, which PBS NewsHour described as Tehran's de facto top decision-making body, said Iran would block "any conditional and limited reopening" of the strait until the United States lifts its port blockade.

Washington's core demand — the handover of Iran's stockpile of enriched uranium — also remains unresolved. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Saeed Khatibzadeh told the Associated Press that Iran will not hand over its 970 pounds of enriched material, which Trump last week called "nuclear dust" and said the United States would "start excavating with big machinery" to recover. Khatibzadeh called the demand "a nonstarter" and said the Americans were "risking the whole ceasefire package."

## On the ground

The war, which began on Feb. 28 with joint U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran, has killed at least 3,000 people in Iran, more than 2,290 in Lebanon, 23 in Israel and more than a dozen in Gulf Arab states, according to figures cited by the Associated Press. Fifteen Israeli soldiers have been killed in Lebanon and 13 U.S. service members across the region. More than 20,000 seafarers remain stranded on hundreds of ships in the Gulf.

The separate ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, which took effect at midnight on Friday, is holding but fraying. A French peacekeeper, Florian Montorio, and two Israeli soldiers were killed in southern Lebanon over the weekend. President Emmanuel Macron said the peacekeeper's death appeared to be caused by Hezbollah fire and described it as an attack on the UN mission; Hezbollah denied responsibility.

Pakistan's role as intermediary has widened. Army Chief Asim Munir traveled to Iran last week, and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said Islamabad was working to "bridge" the differences between the two capitals. A regional official involved in the mediation, who spoke on condition of anonymity, told the Associated Press that U.S. advance security teams were already on the ground.

## The counterpoint

Today's reporting draws on wire and lean-left outlets; administration officials beyond Trump's social-media posts had not publicly elaborated the U.S. position by Sunday evening, and the opposing bucket — Republican lawmakers, Israeli officials and right-leaning outlets — had not weighed in on the second round of talks in the material under review. Abas Aslani, a senior fellow at the Centre for Middle East Strategic Studies in Tehran, told Al Jazeera that the two sides are "engaging in war rhetoric ahead of any possible escalation and military conflict," He added: "It seems that they are pressuring each other to win concessions – and we are not there yet."

The ceasefire deadline falls on Wednesday. Trump said Friday the United States might "have to start dropping bombs again" if the talks fail, while also saying he believes a deal can be reached. Iran's Supreme National Security Council said Tehran was reviewing "new proposals" from Washington that were delivered by Munir during his visit last week.
