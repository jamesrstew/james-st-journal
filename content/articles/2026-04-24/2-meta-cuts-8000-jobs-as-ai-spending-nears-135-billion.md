Meta Platforms will cut roughly 8,000 jobs, about 10 percent of its workforce, with departures starting May 20, the company told employees in an internal memo on Wednesday. It will also freeze 6,000 open requisitions and forecast capital spending of up to $135 billion this year, nearly double the prior year's outlay, with the bulk directed at artificial-intelligence infrastructure and data centers.

The announcement is the largest headcount reduction at the Menlo Park, Calif., company since its 2023 "year of efficiency" and marks the clearest signal yet that Mark Zuckerberg intends to fund Meta's AI buildout by shrinking the rest of the business. Pinterest and Dow Chemical have attributed recent cuts to the same technology, pulling Meta into a widening AI-driven layoff wave across U.S. corporations.

## The memo

Chief people officer Janelle Gale told staff in the memo that the reductions are meant to free cash for other bets. "We're doing this as part of our continued effort to run the company more efficiently and to allow us to offset the other investments we're making," Gale wrote, according to NPR. She called the cuts "unwelcome news" that "puts everyone in an uneasy state," and told employees the decision "is the best path forward, given the circumstances."

CBS News reported that Meta confirmed the May 20 departure date through the same memo. The company has not said how the cuts are distributed across product lines, though it previously eliminated 700 roles in its Reality Labs metaverse division and has been shifting engineers toward generative AI work aimed at rivals OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.

## On the Street

Wedbush Securities analyst Dan Ives told CBS News more cuts are likely before year-end. "We believe that this is part of Meta's strategy to... leverage AI tools to automate tasks that once required large teams, allowing the company to streamline operations and reduce costs while maintaining productivity, driving an increased need for a leaner operating structure," Ives said.

Meta's own framing is more expansive. In a January regulatory filing cited by CBS News, the company said it is working to deliver "personal superintelligence for everyone" and defined superintelligence as "AI that surpasses human intelligence." The $135 billion capital-expenditure forecast is the financial expression of that pitch, and it now rests on a smaller payroll.

## Legal overhang

Two recent jury verdicts sharpened the cost pressure. A New Mexico jury found Meta failed to protect young users from child sexual exploitation, with potential penalties of up to $375 million, NPR reported. A Los Angeles jury awarded $6 million to a plaintiff who alleged mental-health harms from childhood social-media use. Meta plans to appeal both. Neither verdict is final, but together they enlarge the universe of costs the efficiency push is meant to absorb.

## Counterpoint

The company's statements and the source reporting in this dossier both lean left; Meta had not expanded on its efficiency rationale beyond the Gale memo and its January filing by press time, and no center or right-leaning outlet in today's dossier offered an alternate read on whether the $135 billion capex or the layoffs are warranted. Ives's call for further cuts is the only outside voice on the record.

The cut lands in a week thick with AI corporate churn. Apple on Monday named hardware chief John Ternus to succeed Tim Cook on Sept. 1, and SpaceX on Tuesday secured a $60 billion option to buy coding startup Cursor with a $10 billion breakup fee. May 20, when Meta's departures begin, is the next date to watch.
