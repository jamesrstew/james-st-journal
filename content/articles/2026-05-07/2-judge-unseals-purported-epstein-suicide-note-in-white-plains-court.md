U.S. District Judge Kenneth Karas in White Plains, N.Y., on Wednesday ordered the release of a handwritten note that Jeffrey Epstein's former cellmate, Nicholas Tartaglione, said he found after the financier's first suspected jail suicide attempt in July 2019. The Associated Press, in a piece carried by PBS NewsHour, reported that the document had been sealed in a courthouse vault for nearly five years as part of an unrelated legal dispute, and was unsealed after The New York Times petitioned last week.

The note's release puts a long-buried artifact of Epstein's final weeks back into public view at a moment when his estate's victims and their families are pressing on multiple fronts for further accountability, including over the conduct of the former Prince Andrew and the British monarchy's response to it.

## What the note says

The note is short, partly illegible and unsigned. "They investigated me for month -- found nothing!!!" it reads, according to the AP transcription. "It is a treat to be able to choose" the "time to say goodbye," the note continues. "Watcha want me to do -- Bust out cryin!!"

"NO FUN," the note concludes, with those words underlined. "NOT WORTH IT!!"

Tartaglione, a former police officer serving a life sentence for killing four people, claimed he discovered the note in a book in his cell after Epstein was found on July 23, 2019, with a strip of bedsheet around his neck. Few people knew the document existed until Tartaglione mentioned it on a podcast last year.

## Authorship unresolved

The AP reported that it is unclear who wrote the note that Tartaglione claimed he found, and that the document was not mentioned in the lengthy government reports examining the circumstances of Epstein's death. Epstein was found dead in his cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Center in Manhattan on Aug. 10, 2019, while awaiting trial on sex trafficking charges. The medical examiner ruled the death a suicide. Authorities have pointed to a series of missteps by jail personnel, including browsing the internet and sleeping when they should have been checking on Epstein, for allowing him to take his own life.

Ghislaine Maxwell, who prosecutors said helped Epstein recruit and groom victims, is serving a 20-year federal sentence after being convicted in 2021 of sex trafficking and related charges.

## A parallel push in Washington and London

The document release coincided with renewed advocacy from Epstein survivors and their families. Virginia Roberts Giuffre, who accused Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor of sexually abusing her when she was 17, died by suicide last year at 41. Her family was in Washington this week, lobbying as King Charles III completed a four-day state visit marking the 250th anniversary of American independence.

A Buckingham Palace spokesperson told USA Today the king would not meet with Giuffre's brother, citing concerns it could affect "ongoing police inquiries" and potential legal action involving the former Prince Andrew. Rep. Ro Khanna, a California Democrat, had urged the monarch to meet survivors during the Capitol leg of the trip.

Giuffre's brother, Sky Roberts, told Fox News Digital the family was steps from the king's schedule. "I do believe it was a missed opportunity for the king," Roberts said. "We were less than five minutes away from him. We were in D.C. lobbying at the same time the king was there." Roberts added that the family is pressing for "Virginia's Law," legislation Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer introduced on Feb. 10 to eliminate the statute of limitations on civil claims by adult survivors of sex trafficking and abuse.

In October, the king stripped Andrew of his royal titles and evicted him from Royal Lodge after weeks of pressure tied to his relationship with Epstein and renewed allegations in Giuffre's posthumous memoir, "Nobody's Girl." On Feb. 19, his birthday, Andrew was arrested by British police and held for about 11 hours on suspicion of misconduct in public office tied to his Epstein links, then released under investigation, meaning he has neither been charged nor exonerated.

## Counterpoint

The wire and lean-right reporting on the case diverge in posture. The AP file, carried by PBS NewsHour, emphasizes that the note's authorship is unknown and that the document was absent from the official record of Epstein's death, framing the release as a transparency milestone rather than a revelation. Fox News, drawing on extended interviews with Giuffre's family, foregrounds survivor advocacy and what Sky Roberts called the king's "missed opportunity" to stand publicly with victims. Buckingham Palace's stated reason for declining a meeting, the prospect of disturbing live British police inquiries, is the principal counter to that missed-opportunity framing.

## What comes next

Tartaglione's underlying case in White Plains continues, and the Times petition that pried Wednesday's note loose leaves additional sealed filings on the docket. Andrew remains under investigation by British police, and the Giuffre family said it would keep campaigning for Virginia's Law in Washington.
