President Volodymyr Zelensky dismissed Ukraine's top general Tuesday, replacing army commander-in-chief Oleksandr Syrsky with Maj. Gen. Mykhailo Drapatyi in a late-night reshuffle that ended six days of nationwide protests and exposed the deepest political rift inside Kyiv's wartime leadership.

The announcement capped a week that began with the surprise ouster of Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov, the 35-year-old technocrat widely credited with Ukraine's drone-driven battlefield gains. Fedorov's firing last Wednesday triggered rallies in cities across Ukraine, drove the deputy Air Force commander and other senior officers to resign and pushed Zelensky to jettison the general many demonstrators blamed for blocking military reform. Officials described the reshuffle as one of the biggest wartime shakeups since Russia's 2022 invasion.

## The new commander

Drapatyi, 43, commands Ukraine's Joint Forces in Kharkiv Oblast, where troops have logged battlefield progress against Russian forces this year. He was praised for helping halt Russia's 2014 push into eastern Ukraine and in 2024 took command of the units that stopped a Russian advance on Zelensky's hometown of Kryvyi Rih and helped force Russian troops back from parts of the Kherson region, according to Al Jazeera. He resigned as ground forces commander in 2025 after a Russian strike on a training center killed several Ukrainian troops.

"I will work responsibly, with full focus, and with respect for the people who are defending our country today," Drapatyi wrote on Facebook, thanking Syrsky for his work to strengthen the army.

## What ignited the crisis

Zelensky removed Fedorov last Wednesday, citing what he called an irresolvable feud between the defense minister and Syrsky. "I wanted unity very much. The sides did not achieve it. And that isn't just their problem, but mine as well," Zelensky told journalists in Kyiv, according to CBS News. Fedorov, appointed in January, energized the ministry with data-driven analysis of front-line performance, led the "Army of Drones" fundraising campaign and introduced credits that rewarded units for hitting Russian assets.

Syrsky, 60, is viewed by many in Ukraine as a Soviet-school commander wary of the technological overhaul Fedorov pushed, the BBC reported. In a farewell address on Telegram, Syrsky said he was handing his successor a force "not only holding the line but is advancing," and added, "I sincerely hope this offensive will continue."

## Six days of protest

Demonstrators demanded Fedorov's reinstatement and Syrsky's removal for six consecutive days, holding placards and flags in Ukrainian cities. The pro-government media organization United24 suspended publication to join the rallies. A member of parliament from Zelensky's own party told CBS News that "100% we will not accept" Fedorov's replacement.

Zelensky said Tuesday that he met Fedorov earlier in the day and offered him a "worthy position in power" tying together Ukraine's technology capabilities, according to the BBC. He did not disclose whether Fedorov accepted. Fedorov, in a post on X, endorsed Drapatyi's appointment as "a new source of hope in the fight of free people for freedom and justice" and "the voice of change that could no longer be ignored," according to CNBC.

## Moscow shrugs

The Kremlin signaled the reshuffle would not shift its almost four-and-a-half-year offensive. "I don't think this will lead to any changes on the front lines," Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters, according to Al Jazeera. The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine documented 1,396 civilian deaths and 7,978 injuries during the first six months of 2026, a 37 percent increase over the same period a year earlier, mission head Danielle Bell said in Geneva. Russia has intensified ballistic-missile barrages, Ukraine is running short of Patriot interceptors and U.S.-led talks on ending the war remain stalled as Washington concentrates on its parallel war with Iran.

## What comes next

Acting Defense Minister Yevhenii Khmara, a former Security Service official installed after Fedorov's ouster, remains in his post for now. Zelensky has asked Khmara and Drapatyi to draft a new defense strategy covering the military corps system, weapons supply and drones. Whether that agenda pulls Fedorov back into government, and defuses the political revolt his firing set off, will shape the next phase of Ukraine's wartime summer.
