The Democratic National Committee on Saturday approved a 2028 presidential primary calendar that keeps South Carolina in the leadoff position and pushes Iowa out of the party's early window entirely. South Carolina will vote first on Jan. 22, 2028, followed by Nevada on Feb. 1, New Hampshire on Feb. 8, New Mexico on Feb. 15, Michigan on Feb. 22 and Virginia on Feb. 29.

The vote, taken at the DNC's summer meeting in Austin, locks in a rearrangement the party first attempted for 2024 and now intends to defend. It hands early influence to a Democratic electorate that is disproportionately Black, Latino and union-organized, and it forces the party's next presidential field to court those constituencies before winning a single delegate in a majority-white state.

## Who votes first

South Carolina's Black voters have decided the last three contested Democratic nominations. Barack Obama in 2008, Hillary Clinton in 2016 and Joe Biden in 2020 each carried the state's primary, where Black voters can yield more than half of the Democratic electorate. Nevada, home to the service-industry unions of Las Vegas, and New Mexico, a border state with a large Latino population, follow before New Hampshire gets its turn.

The calendar then moves to Michigan, anchored by the auto industry, and Virginia, home to more federal workers than any other state. All but South Carolina among the six early states have been competitive in recent general elections, giving Democratic candidates a combined 43 Electoral College votes' worth of ground game before Super Tuesday.

## The party's argument

DNC Chair Ken Martin said the sequence is designed to test candidates against the coalition Democrats actually need. "This is much bigger than a lineup of dates on a calendar," Martin said. "This is about our approach to winning back the presidency." He said the eventual nominee "will have to earn the support across the full breadth of America. Every region, every demographic, every generation."

Democratic strategist Karen Finney, who has worked on multiple presidential campaigns, said the schedule forces candidates to demonstrate range. "They have to show that they can go to the South and talk to Black voters and rural voters, as well as out west, talking ranching and mining issues, to a border state, to union members," Finney said. "Whoever survives will be battle-tested."

Howard Dean, the former Vermont governor and DNC chair who first added Nevada and South Carolina to the early window, said the new lineup is a further improvement. "These states look more like the country," Dean said, "whereas Iowa and New Hampshire don't." Strategist Chuck Rocha told CBS News that under the new schedule a serious candidate must be "strong with Latino voters or...strong with Black voters."

## Costs and logistics

Party officials say the compressed geography also cuts campaign costs in ways that could help lesser-known candidates. South Carolina's smaller media markets are cheaper than New Hampshire's Boston-adjacent airwaves, and its footprint spills into North Carolina and Georgia. "They can campaign from the top to the bottom in a day," said Bre Maxwell, a DNC member from South Carolina.

The clustered map also offers fundraising benefits. A western swing pairs Nevada and New Mexico with a California donor stop. A Virginia visit puts candidates within reach of Washington television studios. A New Hampshire trip can be routed through New York City.

## The dissent

Iowa Democratic Party Chair Rita Hart, whose state has been dropped from the early states entirely, said the calendar "fundamentally misses the mark" and warned it sidelines Midwestern voters Democrats need to win. New Hampshire Democratic Party Chair Ray Buckley called the demotion "disappointing." The state's congressional delegation argued that the New Hampshire primary is "a tested proving ground" that "forges better candidates and better presidents."

New Hampshire defied the DNC's schedule in 2024 by voting first anyway, and the party stripped its sanctioned status in response. Martin said future violations will face "more severe" punishment. "This is a calendar this party is actually going to protect," he said. Republicans, who are expected to leave Iowa atop their own 2028 calendar, were not represented in Saturday's DNC proceedings or in the wire reporting from Austin.

## What comes next

The 2028 Democratic field has yet to form, though potential candidates have already been visiting South Carolina. The first votes will be cast on Jan. 22, 2028 — a little more than 17 months after Saturday's decision.
