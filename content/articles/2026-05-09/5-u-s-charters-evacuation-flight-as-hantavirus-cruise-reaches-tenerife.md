The U.S. government is dispatching a Centers for Disease Control and Prevention repatriation flight to the Canary Islands to retrieve 17 Americans from the hantavirus-stricken MV Hondius, which is due to anchor off Tenerife at dawn Sunday local time after a run north from Cape Verde. The Dutch-flagged ship, barred from docking by Canary Islands authorities, will instead transfer passengers offshore to small boats, then to sealed buses, then to waiting national charter aircraft on the airport runway, Spanish officials said Friday.

Friday's edition reported Spain's preparations to receive the more than 140 passengers and crew. What is new is the U.S. extraction plan, the disembarkation choreography and a CDC field deployment, all hardening around a vessel that has carried a rare, occasionally person-to-person virus across the Atlantic for more than a month.

## The American leg

The Americans will fly to Offutt Air Force Base in Omaha and then move to the National Quarantine Unit at the University of Nebraska Medical Center, the CDC and the university said. Michael Wadman, the unit's medical director, said the team is preparing for 17 to 19 arrivals. "Each individual will have their own room," Wadman told reporters Friday, adding that rooms resemble hotel rooms with WiFi and exercise equipment for what he said is an undefined quarantine window. A second CDC team will staff Offutt; a third is already in the Canary Islands to run exposure assessments passenger by passenger.

## Ship to runway

Virginia Balcones, secretary general of Spain's civil protection service, said disembarkation will run country by country, in groups of five, only after passengers are confirmed asymptomatic. Balcones added: "There will be no contact with civilian personnel." Medicalized aircraft are on standby in case symptoms emerge in transit, though the working assumption is standard charters. World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus is expected on the island to coordinate, according to Spanish ministry sources cited by Agence France-Presse. After offload, the Hondius is to sail home to the Netherlands with a skeleton crew.

## The case math

The outbreak has produced nine confirmed or suspected cases and three deaths, including a Dutch couple who had spent months bird-watching in Argentina, Uruguay and Chile, regions that carry the Andes strain, the only hantavirus known to spread person to person. None of the 147 people on board was symptomatic Friday, the WHO and Spanish officials said. More than a dozen countries are tracking earlier disembarkers.

The dossier carries no center-wire perspective; both U.S. outlets lean partisan, and the WHO and Spanish authorities appear only through their statements. President Trump, quoted by Fox News on Friday, said the situation "seems to be okay" and that the virus is "Not easily transferable, unlike COVID." Angela Hewlett, medical director of the Nebraska Biocontainment Unit, was more guarded. "I do not see this as progressing to a worldwide pandemic, although there's still a lot of unknowns, and I think we all need to recognize that," Hewlett said at a news conference. Spain's Secretary of State for Health Javier Padilla said the situation "is of very low risk for the general population," echoing earlier government messaging.

The first small boats are expected to leave the Hondius between Sunday and Monday. The Nebraska quarantine clock, once it starts, has no fixed end.
