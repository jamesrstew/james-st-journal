Apple and Intel have reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture some of the processors that go into Apple devices, the Wall Street Journal reported Friday, sending Intel shares up nearly 14 percent on the day and capping a week in which Wall Street rotated out of Nvidia and into the rest of the chip stack.

The report, which both companies declined to confirm, would end Apple's exclusive reliance on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. for its most advanced silicon and hand Intel's long-troubled foundry business its first marquee external customer. Intel is up more than 200 percent for 2026. Nvidia, the world's most valuable company, is up 15 percent.

## What shifted

Mizuho analyst Jordan Klein described the week as a possible "changing of the guard in AI." Advanced Micro Devices and Intel each gained about 25 percent on the week, memory maker Micron jumped more than 37 percent, and fiber-optic cable maker Corning climbed about 18 percent, according to CNBC. All four have more than doubled this year. Nvidia rallied 8 percent on the week but trails the group for 2026.

The sequence began Tuesday, when Bloomberg reported Apple was in talks with Intel and Samsung to produce U.S.-made processors. Intel rose 13 percent that day and another 14 percent on Friday's scoop. Apple gained 2 percent.

## On the foundry

Intel's Chandler, Arizona, plant is in high-volume production on its 18A node, meant to rival TSMC's 2-nanometer process. Creative Strategies analyst Ben Bajarin told CNBC that Apple is more likely to use Intel's next-generation 18A-P node, which could scale as soon as next year. He called 18A "a little bit rough" and said 18A-P "cleans a lot of stuff up."

"Intel is the only place that can scale up capacity as a viable second source," Bajarin said. He added that the deal would not dent TSMC, because the Taiwanese foundry is "already printing wafers as fast as they can."

## CPUs and memory

Bank of America estimates the data-center CPU market could more than double from $27 billion in 2025 to $60 billion in 2030 as workloads shift from chatbots to AI agents. AMD this week reported results ahead of estimates and said it now expects 35 percent annual server-CPU growth over the next three to five years, up from 18 percent in November. Goldman Sachs and Bernstein upgraded the stock to buy.

Micron crossed an $800 billion market capitalization for the first time this week. Chief Executive Sanjay Mehrotra told CNBC in March that key customers are getting only "50% to two-thirds of their requirements."

## The caveat

Apple and Intel both declined to comment on the reported deal, which is sourced to people familiar with the matter. The Verge noted it remains "still unclear" which Apple products would carry Intel-made chips, and Intel Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan has said the company's 14A node will not be in volume production until 2029. BTIG analyst Jonathan Krinsky warned this week of a possible 25 percent to 30 percent correction in the PHLX Semiconductor Index, up 66 percent this year, saying the move "in many cases" has not been seen since the dot-com bubble.

Nvidia's next quarterly earnings report will be the next test of whether the rotation sticks.
