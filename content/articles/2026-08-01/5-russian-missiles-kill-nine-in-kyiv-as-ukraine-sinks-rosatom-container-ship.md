Russian ballistic missiles killed at least nine people and wounded 28 in Kyiv early Saturday, Ukrainian officials said, and hours later President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukrainian drones had sunk the Russian-owned container ship Yanina in the Black Sea in retaliation.

The exchange extends a monthslong intensification of long-range strikes that the United Nations said made June the deadliest month for Ukrainian civilians since April 2022, and it lands as Zelensky publicly presses President Trump to grant Ukraine a license to build the Patriot interceptors that are Kyiv's only defense against Russian ballistic missiles.

## Inside the city

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said more than 10 explosions shook the capital, setting off fires and blackouts. In the Darnytskyi district, seven people were killed and 14 wounded, including two children, the State Emergency Service said. In Solomyanskyi, two people were killed and eight wounded, including two children, when a five-story residential building was partially destroyed. A missile landed near the Lithuanian embassy, breaking windows and scattering debris in the compound, Lithuania's foreign ministry said.

## Black Sea strike

Zelensky said on X that the 100,000-tonne Yanina, owned by Russia's state nuclear operator Rosatom, had been "sent to the bottom" early Saturday. All 17 crew members abandoned the vessel about 130 miles from the Russian port of Novorossiysk, according to Stsana Rosatom, a Russian nuclear-industry outlet, which said the crew "were in good condition." Rosatom said the ship had been carrying frozen food and construction materials, and Chief Executive Alexey Likhachev called the strike "piracy and robbery."

## Patriots in play

Zelensky met Trump in Washington earlier this week to press for the Patriot license. At a Camp David cabinet meeting Friday, Trump said talks were continuing but that Washington had not agreed, calling the transfer "a big step" and warning that "those people that you give that technology, they can someday turn on you." Zelensky wrote on X on Friday, after a call he described as "good" with Vice President JD Vance, that "air defence – specifically Patriot interceptors against ballistic missiles – remains a top priority."

## Wider exchange

Saturday's strikes follow a Thursday Russian barrage that killed at least eight Ukrainian civilians and dropped what Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said was probably a Kh-101 cruise missile into a field in eastern Poland, and last Friday's strike on a Kyiv defense-industry gathering that killed 10. Ukraine has widened its own campaign against Russian oil depots and against warehouses of the online retailer Wildberries, which Kyiv accuses of storing drone parts for the Russian army. A Wildberries facility in Volgograd caught fire after an overnight attack Friday, the company said.

Rosatom's condemnation was the only Russian response cited in the BBC and Al Jazeera accounts of Saturday's strikes, and the Kremlin had not commented on the Kyiv attack by press time. Trump's Camp David remarks framed Patriot co-production as a technology-transfer risk rather than a Ukrainian need, and walked back his July offer to grant the license. Russian President Vladimir Putin acknowledged in June that Ukrainian strikes were causing "problems" for Russia but described the effect as "a certain shortage, but it's not critical."

Zelensky's next move on Patriot licensing hinges on further contact with Washington after the Vance call; Trump left the decision open Friday and set no deadline.
